Pool fencing regulations in Victoria

State rules, inspection steps, electrical bonding and official council links—explained in plain English.

The short answer

Five duties every pool owner should know.

Your council’s registration letter identifies the construction date, applicable standard and certificate due date for the property.

01

More than 30 cm

A pool or spa that can hold more than 30 centimetres of water needs a compliant barrier.

02

Register with council

The owner must register the pool or spa with the relevant local council.

03

Use the right standard

The applicable barrier rules depend on the pool’s construction date and later alterations.

04

Inspect every four years

An authorised practitioner must inspect the barrier on the required cycle.

05

Lodge the certificate

The owner lodges the compliance certificate with council, generally within 30 days of issue.

Which barrier rules apply?

Start with the pool’s construction date.

Council determines the construction date from its records and tells the owner which standard applies. Do not choose a checklist based only on how old the fence looks.

Before 8 April 1991Use the official checklist council identifies for this period.
8 April 1991–30 April 2010A different set of barrier provisions can apply.
From 1 May 2010Use the current construction-date category confirmed by council.

Later alterations can affect the applicable standard. A replacement barrier may also need a building permit, even when the original pool is older. Confirm the pathway before removal or construction.

Electrical bonding

Metal near a pool needs an electrician’s assessment.

People often call this “earthing”, but the specific safety measure is equipotential bonding. It reduces dangerous voltage differences between conductive parts if an electrical fault occurs.

Under the national Wiring Rules, conductive metal within arm’s reach of the pool edge—often treated as 1.25 metres—can require bonding. This may include metal fencing, posts, glass-fence spigots, ladders, reinforcing steel, light poles and shade structures.

  • Ask a licensed electrician to assess the design before posts, spigots or concrete are installed.
  • Only a licensed electrician can install, alter, connect and test electrical bonding.
  • Do not cut, remove or cover an existing green-and-yellow bonding conductor.
  • RJL coordinates the fence layout; the electrician controls electrical compliance and certification.

Before inspection

Keep the gate and the space around it clear.

The formal checklist depends on the construction date, but these everyday checks are useful for every pool area:

  • Make sure each gate closes and latches by itself from every open position.
  • Never prop a pool gate open.
  • Move furniture, pots, toys, pumps and stored items away from climbable areas.
  • Trim plants that create footholds or stop the gate from closing.
  • Check for movement, corrosion, broken panels and changing gaps under the barrier.
  • Use the exact self-assessment checklist linked by your council.

These checks do not replace a formal inspection.

Pool fencing questions

Clear answers to common searches.

Which pools and spas need a safety barrier in Victoria?
A swimming pool or spa capable of containing more than 30 centimetres of water requires a compliant safety barrier, including many relocatable pools.
How often must a Victorian pool barrier be inspected?
After registration, owners must arrange a barrier inspection and lodge a certificate of barrier compliance on the cycle set by Victorian law, generally every four years.
Who can certify a pool fence in Victoria?
Formal inspection and certification must be completed by an appropriately registered building surveyor, building inspector or authorised pool safety inspector. A fence installer does not automatically have authority to certify it.
Does a metal pool fence need earthing?
Metal pool fencing and other conductive parts close to the pool may require equipotential bonding under the Wiring Rules. Only a licensed electrician can assess, install, alter and test the bonding work.
Do councils have different pool fence standards?
Victorian law sets the framework. Councils manage local registration, due dates, certificate lodgement, fees and enforcement. The applicable barrier standard is tied mainly to the pool construction date and later alterations, not a council-created design standard.
