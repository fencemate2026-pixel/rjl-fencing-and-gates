Fencing a sloping block in Melbourne

Compare stepped and raked boundary fences on a sloping Melbourne block. Plan ground gaps, retaining walls and what to show your fencing contractor.

RJL homeowner advicePublished 17 November 2026RJL Fencing and Gates · Research-assisted guideAll advice ↗

The first decision on a sloping block is what you want the top and bottom of the fence to do. A level top in each bay gives a stepped profile. A raked fence follows the fall of the ground with angled rails or panels suited to the system. Both can provide privacy, but they leave different gaps, need different detailing and can look quite different from the neighbour’s side.

Read the slope from both properties

Walk the fence line from your side and note where the ground changes level. Take a wide photo from each end and extra photos around corners, retaining walls and proposed gates. If you have permission to photograph from the other side, capture that view too. An existing fence can look level from one yard while leaving a much larger gap over the lower yard. Keep any rough measurements separate from the surveyed boundary. If the position of the legal boundary is unclear, resolve it before agreeing where the fence will go.

Compare stepped and raked panels on the same sketch

Stepped bays suit a layout where you want a horizontal top in each section. Ask the installer to show where the height changes and what happens under the downhill end of each bay. A raked line can give a steadier clearance on a consistent slope. On uneven ground, you may need different treatment where the fall changes direction or meets a gate. Ask for a simple side elevation showing the ground, posts, top line and biggest gap underneath each section.

BlueScope’s fencing installation guide illustrates raking on sloping ground. BlueScope also recommends getting the installation guidance for your particular fence from its supplier. Ask which system the quote covers and whether the proposed profile is supported on your site. COLORBOND® steel fencing guidance.

Agree how to handle ground gaps

If you have a dog, tell the installer how small it is and where it can reach the bottom edge from both sides. Nominate the gaps you are most concerned about and ask how they will be addressed. Don’t assume that topping up soil will solve the problem. Panel clearance, drainage and ground support all need to be considered together. If any part of the fence will also act as a pool or spa barrier, show that location at the start and ask your council or building surveyor about the separate barrier requirements for your property.

Treat retaining as a separate decision

If soil stands higher on one side of the proposed line, identify what will support it. Ask who is checking the condition and suitability of any existing wall before posts are fixed to it or placed alongside it. A fence replacement can expose a wall already under pressure, and a fence quote may cover the panels and posts without covering wall repairs or drainage. In Victoria, the Building and Plumbing Commission identifies walls at least 1 m high and walls on or near boundaries where adjoining property could be damaged as requiring building permits. Confirm the requirements for your property with your council or building surveyor before work starts. Victorian Building and Plumbing Commission guidance.

Checklist before requesting a quote

  • Send your suburb and photos showing the full fence line, changes in ground level and any wall or gate.
  • Ask for the proposed steps or rake, the worst gap below the fence and the clearance at gate openings.
  • Confirm who will assess retaining, drainage and any project approvals.
  • Agree who will obtain utility plans before excavation and check what further locating is needed. BYDA advises that its plans are indicative and may lack detail about services within private property. Before You Dig Australia homeowner guidance.
  • Compare quotes against the same sketch and ask which parts of the site work are included.
