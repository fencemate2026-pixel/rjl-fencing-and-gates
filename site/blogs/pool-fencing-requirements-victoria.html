Pool fencing requirements in Victoria: a homeowner checklist

A practical Victorian homeowner guide to pool barriers, registration, inspections and planning a compliant replacement fence.

RJL homeowner advicePublished 21 August 2026Discuss your site ↗All advice ↗

In Victoria, swimming pools and spas capable of holding more than 30 centimetres of water require a compliant safety barrier. The exact standard that applies can depend on when the pool or spa was installed.

Your local council records the registration and certificate due dates. Before changing an existing barrier, confirm the applicable requirements with council or a registered building surveyor.

Start with these checks

  • Confirm that the pool or spa is registered with the local council.
  • Check the due date for the next barrier inspection and certificate.
  • Keep gates self-closing and self-latching, with no obstruction to their movement.
  • Remove climbable objects and vegetation from the non-climbable zone.
  • Ask before altering a barrier because a building permit may be required.

Design compliance and appearance together

A compliant barrier can still suit the architecture of the home. Glass, vertical batten, aluminium and custom steel systems each change privacy, sightlines, maintenance and the way the pool area feels.

Victorian pool and spa safety guidance ↗

Plan electrical bonding before metalwork is installed

Metal fencing, posts and glass-fence spigots close to a pool can require equipotential bonding under the Wiring Rules. A licensed electrician must assess and complete the electrical work. Plan this before concrete is poured or metalwork is fixed in place.
